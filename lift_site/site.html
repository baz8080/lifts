What this measures

This site reads Irish Rail's public service-message feed every 30 minutes — the banners shown on irishrail.ie — and keeps every notice that says a lift or escalator is out of order. Irish Rail publishes no history, so nothing before exists here, and days before it are shown as “no data” rather than as working lifts.

A notice going up is the only signal, and a notice coming down is the only other one. The feed has no “fixed” field: an outage ends here when its notice is no longer listed, which usually means the lift is back but strictly means only that Irish Rail took the notice down. Notices have been seen to appear and disappear in batches, months after the start date they carry.

How to read an outage

Each outage shows when its notice was first listed and when it was no longer listed — or “still listed at the last poll” for one that is up now — and the duration between the two, to the nearest half hour. That is what the day bars colour. The notice also carries a start date of Irish Rail's own, shown beside it as their claim: it is routinely weeks or months before the notice was listed, over days this site was watching and the notice was not there, so it colours nothing here.

“Planned works” is what the notice itself says — temporarily unavailable due to planned works — against currently out of service for everything else. Escalators are listed in the same feed with the same wording and are shown too, tagged separately.

Irish Rail sometimes reissues a notice with new wording or a corrected start. When the new one appears at the very poll the old one vanished, they are shown as one outage with the reissue noted. A notice that comes back a poll or more later is a separate outage — the gap is real, and it is what this site is measuring.

What it cannot tell you

How many lifts a station has, or whether the one that is out is the only route to a platform: the notice names platforms in prose and nothing more. Whether a lift is back in service before its notice is removed, or still out after. Anything shorter than half an hour. And whether an outage that begins and ends between two polls happened at all.

Each notice carries an end date, but for nearly every one it is a placeholder near the end of the year. It is shown as “Irish Rail's listed end” for what it is worth and plays no part in anything measured.
